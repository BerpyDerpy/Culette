# Culette — Design Direction & First Build Priorities

## 1. Design philosophy

**Scientific minimalism meets industrial sophistication.**

Culette should feel like an advanced engineering company working at the intersection of mathematical abstraction and the physical world. Its identity should communicate precision, curiosity, intelligence and the ability to turn complex systems into practical solutions.

The visual language should draw from three connected ideas:

- **Generative mathematics:** recursive geometry, crystalline forms, parametric curves and carefully composed mathematical patterns.
- **Engineering precision:** technical drawings, cross-sections, measurements, structured layouts and purposeful annotations.
- **Intelligent physical systems:** robotics, autonomous machines, environmental data and real-world engineering.

These are not separate themes. They are different expressions of one philosophy: understand the system, then engineer the solution.

Avoid generic AI imagery, excessive futuristic decoration, glowing circuit patterns and visual effects that do not communicate anything meaningful.

## 2. Logo direction

Refine the existing Culette mark rather than replacing its identity.

The current open-C symbol is a useful foundation. Evolve it into a more deliberate geometric emblem:

- Preserve the recognisable open-C silhouette.
- Use subtle facets or planes inspired by a precisely cut gemstone.
- Keep the central line or point as a restrained reference to a culet, a focal point or a connection.
- Make the symbol simple enough to remain recognisable at small sizes.
- Keep the wordmark clean, calm and contemporary.
- Prefer a flat, high-contrast version for everyday use; metallic or dimensional treatments should be reserved for selected brand moments.

The gemstone connection should be an influence, not a literal jewellery theme. The mark should communicate precision and geometry rather than luxury retail.

## 3. Colour and typography

### Primary palette

- Obsidian black: `#080A0E`
- Graphite: `#151922`
- Warm ivory: `#EAE8E1`
- Technical silver: `#B6BDC6`
- Signal amber: `#E99A38`
- Restrained signal red: `#C94E2B`

Use dark neutrals as the foundation, warm light typography for clarity, and amber sparingly for emphasis.

Individual projects may use their own supporting colours while retaining Culette's overall visual identity:

- Rover: industrial orange and metal.
- Computational engineering: thermal colours and restrained technical blues.
- Ocea: ocean blue, cyan and teal.

Use a modern, understated sans-serif for headlines and body text. A monospaced style can distinguish measurements, equations, coordinates and technical labels. Typography should feel precise, not science-fictional.

## 4. What to build first

### Priority 1 — Establish the identity

Settle the logo proportions, wordmark, colour palette, typography and basic visual rules. Create versions that work on dark and light backgrounds, as well as a symbol-only version.

### Priority 2 — Explore the signature visual

Develop a small set of generative geometric directions for the homepage: recursive branching, crystalline geometry and parametric forms.

Choose one coherent direction. It should be slow, restrained and visually distinctive, not an attention-grabbing screensaver. Where appropriate, let the geometry reveal the mathematical idea behind it.

### Priority 3 — Build the homepage narrative

The homepage should answer three questions quickly:

1. What does Culette do?
2. What makes its approach distinctive?
3. What has it built?

Recommended order:

1. **Hero:** A clear positioning statement, a restrained generative visual and a route into the work.
2. **Capabilities:** Artificial intelligence, robotics and autonomy, computational engineering, and research and development.
3. **Selected work:** The three projects, presented as substantial visual case studies.
4. **Approach:** A concise explanation of the recurring process: Model → Simulate → Build → Iterate.
5. **Research:** Selected experiments, technical work and ideas.
6. **About and contact:** The company, its ambitions and an invitation to collaborate.

Keep the homepage editorial and spacious. It should introduce the company rather than reproduce its full legal objects or service catalogue.

### Priority 4 — Present the three projects

Give each project its own visual character while keeping a consistent Culette framework.

**Autonomous rover — Industrial autonomy**

Lead with the actual machine. Show its form, components, sensing, movement and intended operating environment. Make it feel tangible and engineered.

**Computational engineering — Scientific computation**

Lead with the design process and its outputs: geometry, cooling channels, flow, thermal behaviour and optimization. Use genuine engineering visualizations wherever possible. The project should demonstrate computational depth, not rely on generic rocket imagery.

**Ocea — Environmental intelligence**

Lead with the ocean as a dynamic system. Show 3D ocean data, currents, depth layers, observations and the relationship between forecasts and real measurements. Use actual product imagery and visualizations rather than generic ocean photography.

### Priority 5 — Add depth after the core experience works

Once the identity, homepage and project stories are coherent, expand the research section, add more detailed project material and introduce carefully chosen interactive demonstrations.

Do not let secondary animations or decorative sections delay the core story.

## 5. Animation and imagery principles

Animation should explain, reveal or clarify something.

Good directions include:

- Geometric forms assembling, branching or changing according to simple rules.
- Engineering designs moving from an initial model to a refined result.
- Mechanical components articulating or separating to reveal their structure.
- Ocean currents and data fields evolving over time.
- Technical annotations appearing alongside the relevant visual.

Keep movement measured and purposeful. Text must remain easy to read, and the experience should not depend on animation to communicate essential information.

Prefer real project imagery, genuine simulations and original visualizations over stock technology imagery.

## 6. Layout and tone

The overall layout should feel editorial, spacious and composed:

- Strong, simple headlines.
- Generous negative space.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Asymmetric compositions where they improve the story.
- Technical details used selectively as supporting texture.
- Consistent navigation and structure across all project pages.

The tone should be confident, curious and precise. Avoid inflated claims, excessive corporate language and unsupported descriptions of capabilities.

## 7. The guiding idea

**Understand the system. Engineer the solution.**

A mathematical structure, a rocket engine, an autonomous rover and an ocean model may appear unrelated. Culette brings them together through the same interest in understanding complex systems and building ways to work with them.

That connection—not a particular animation, colour or visual trend—should hold the brand together.
